1. Introduction
e
As supply chain collaboration models gradually replace traditional vertical integration to
become the core organizational form of modern industrial systems, the overall cwompetitiveness of
supply chains has emerged as a key indicator for measuring a nation's economic resilience and
e
development level. However, enterprises across the supply chain—particularly small and medium-
sized businesses—universally face constraints from a lack of long-term financing sources during
i
v
their development, creating a significant bottleneck that hinders the overall efficiency of industrial
chains. Against this backdrop, patient capital—with its long-teerm orientation, risk tolerance, and
deep engagement—offers new possibilities for addressing supply chain financing challenges.
r
Therefore, examining the enabling mechanisms and transm ission pathways of patient capital within
r
supply chains holds significant theoretical and practical implications for enhancing the resilience
e
and security of industrial and supply chains.
Existing theories have provided prelimeinary logical support for the potential spillover effects
of patient capital within supply chains. Against the backdrop of structural limitations in traditional
p
bank credit systems—such as maturity mismatches and risk aversion—patient capital demonstrates
unique complementary financing functions through its long-term orientation and high risk tolerance
t
(Zheng et al., 2000; Zhang oet al., 2017). From a theoretical perspective, patient capital can reduce
information asymmetry between enterprises and financial institutions through its “certification
n
effect,” thereby improving credit allocation efficiency (Li and Ma, 2014). Simultaneously, its
“governance effect” optimizes corporate financial decision-making and financing behavior
t
(Xiong et nal., 2020; Lei and Liu, 2017). These studies provide a theoretical foundation for
understanding the transmission mechanisms of patient capital within supply chain networks,
i
suggesrting it may generate cross-firm spillover effects by alleviating financing constraints among
p
node enterprises.
Against this backdrop, this paper utilizes data on the top five suppliers and customers disclosed
e
by Chinese A-share listed companies, combined with patient capital investment information. By
r
constructing a supply chain-capital linkage sample, it empirically examines the spillover effects of
P
patient capital within supply chains and its underlying mechanisms. The findings reveal that patient
capital can effectively alleviate financing constraints faced by upstream and downstream enterprises
in supply chains through two pathways: accelerating the turnover speed of suppliers' accounts

receivable and increasing the credit loan ratio of customers.
e
This study contributes in two ways. First, it expands the research scope of the economic
consequences of patient capital, extending the focus from individual enterprises tow the supply chain
level. Second, this research provides empirical evidence on the supply chain spillover effects of
e
patient capital, thereby contributing to the understanding of how capital moves across different
entities in supply chain.

2. Theoretical analysis and research hypotheses
The literature on patient capital has primarily evolved aelong two trajectories: one stream
focuses on constructing measurement frameworks from either micro-enterprise or macro-level
r
perspectives (Lin and Wang, 2017; Wu et al., 2022), w hile the other investigates its economic
r
consequences, such as alleviating financing constraints and stimulating corporate innovation (Jiang
e
and Wu, 2024; Yang and Tang, 2025). These studies provide a foundational understanding of patient
capital’s role and lay important groundweork for our further exploration of its transmission
mechanisms within supply chains.
p
While existing literature offers support for patient capital's role in alleviating financing
constraints, its potential spillover effects along the supply chain remain unclear. This paper posits
t
that patient capital can genoerate such spillovers through two key mechanisms: the financing cost
effect, whereby reduced capital costs for invested firms lessen their reliance on excessive trade
n
credit from suppliers, and the credit expansion effect, whereby these firms act as informational
bridges, lowering information asymmetry between financial institutions and their supply chain
t
partners, thnereby enhancing credit access for the latter. Consequently, we propose the core
hypothesis:
i
Hr1: Patient capital investment generates supply chain spillover effects that significantly
p
alleviate the financing constraints of major suppliers and customers.

5.3. Robustness tests
We conducted a series of robustness checks to ensure the validity of our baseline results. First,
e
using stable equity and relational debt as alternative measures of patient capital yielded consistently
r
positive results. Second, employing SA and FC financing constraint indices as proxy variables for
P
financing constraints produced consistent findings. Third, regressions incorporating high-
dimensional fixed effects and varying sample intervals yielded consistent results.
Furthermore, to mitigate potential endogeneity issues, we undertook the following steps: First,

6. Conclusions
r
This study finds that patient capital significantly alle viates financing constraints for upstream
r
and downstream firms in the supply chain through financing cost and credit expansion effects, with
e
these spillover effects being more pronounced in contexts of higher supply chain dependency or
weaker financing capacity among supply cheain partners. Furthermore, mechanism tests confirm that
patient capital enhances investment efficiency and innovation output in supply chain firms by easing
p
financing constraints. These findings underscore the importance of patient capital in optimizing
supply chain resource allocation and promoting industrial upgrading. Future research could extend
t
to non-listed supply chain ofirms and explore the differential impacts of various types of patient
capital.
