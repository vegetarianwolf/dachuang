八、结论与政策启示

随着大数据和数字技术的普及，量化交易已经成为资本市场上不可忽视的重要力
量。量化交易既有可能发挥提供流动性、提高定价效率的作用，也有可能造成趋同交
易、逆向选择等负面影响。如何正确认识量化交易对金融市场稳定的作用具有重要的
理论价值与现实意义。本文利用高频逐笔委托数据从多个维度识别量化交易行为，构
建量化交易指数，探究量化交易对市场—个股和个股—市场两个方向尾部风险传染的
影响。

| 股对市场的尾部风险传染。量化交易活跃度每上升 |     |     |     |     |     | 个标准差，市场冲击引发个股极端风 |     |     |     |     |     |

个标准差。量化交易的正面作用与其提供流动性、加快价格发现的能力有关。我们发现
在股票信息质量较高、股价被低估以及投资者情绪较低时，量化交易抑制市场—个股风险

增强，传染性效应减弱。最后，本文分析了量化交易监管的政策效应，发现加强量化交易

总体看，量化交易既不是单纯的“洪水猛兽”，也并非天然的“稳定器”，其对金融市场
稳定的影响具有显著的条件性和情景依赖性，监管部门需要在防范传染性效应与保持流

基于以上发现，本文的政策启示和建议如下。
第一，提升上市公司信息披露质量，优化量化交易发挥正向作用的基础条件。本文的
研究结论表明，量化交易在公司信息透明度较高的情形下，更能发挥流动性供给和加快价
格发现的积极作用。因此，上市公司应进一步提升披露内容的结构化与规范化水平，减少
令量化模型难以识别或容易产生误判的“噪声信息”。例如，可以在业务分部、运营指标等
方面采用更稳定的披露口径和更细致的指标说明，保持关键经营变量在不同报告期之间
的一致性和可比性；在重大经营调整、业务模式变化等事件发生时，对相关变量的口径变
化和影响路径作出更清晰的说明。这类改进有助于量化模型更准确地提取与基本面相关
的信号，降低由信息噪声和口径变化引发的错误定价，从而让量化交易在系统性冲击下更
好地发挥稳定市场的作用。
第二，促进行业自律与券商执行改进，推动量化策略结构更加稳健。本文研究显
示，不同类型的量化策略对尾部风险传染的影响具有显著差异，部分易引发日内高波动
的策略更可能放大个股向市场的风险扩散。一方面，量化机构应完善内部风控制度，加
强对策略同质性、模型稳健性、极端行情响应能力等方面的评估，主动优化策略结构，减
少短期共振和拥堵风险。行业协会也可推动建立适度的策略透明度规则，如披露策略
大类、主要风险暴露、典型订单执行特征等非敏感信息，以帮助市场更好识别不同策略
的风险特征。另一方面，券商作为执行通道的关键环节，可在执行算法中加入拥堵识
别、智能限速等功能，减少瞬时执行挤兑造成的市场冲击。更高质量的执行服务不仅有
利于提高市场的流动性，也能降低策略间的无效竞争，为量化交易的平稳运行提供
支持。
第三，深化对市场运行机制的理解，提升市场在极端情形下的风险承载能力。本文的
结果表明，量化交易在流动性压力时期能够缓解市场与个股之间的尾部风险传染，而在价
格快速调整时期的稳定性作用较弱，这说明市场在不同状态下对冲击的承载能力会影响
量化交易的作用方向。基于这一机制特征，可以从更宏观的角度理解市场结构在市场稳
定性中的作用：当市场的价格形成过程更加平稳、订单深度更具连续性时，量化交易的流
动性供给优势更容易发挥；而在价格跳跃频繁、市场状态切换较快的情形下，策略同步化
和短期冲击更容易相互强化，从而加剧个别时点的风险共振。因此，在优化市场运行机制
时，应重点关注市场状态与量化交易的交互影响，提升市场在不同条件下吸收冲击的
能力。
第四，优化投资者结构，提升市场在情绪扰动下的稳定性。本文研究发现，当长期资
金占比更高、投资者结构更稳健时，量化交易的正面作用更加突出。长期资金在投资过程
中更加注重基本面和长期价值，其交易行为相对稳定，不会在短期情绪波动中形成“跟风
式”的集体反应。因此，推动长期资金扩大在不同板块和不同市值公司的覆盖度，不仅能
够提升价格发现质量，也能在系统性冲击时期缓解情绪型交易形成的同步化趋势，使市场

对短期波动的敏感度降低。随着投资者结构的优化，量化交易在极端行情中更可能表现
为“流动性缓冲器”，而非风险“放大器”，有助于整体市场生态达到更均衡的状态。
第五，加强投资者教育，提升市场对量化交易的理性认知。量化交易在市场中的影响
往往被部分投资者误解，极端波动时期更容易出现情绪化的归因与非理性行为。本文研
究显示，投资者情绪越高涨，量化交易越可能放大尾部风险传染。因此，改进投资者对量
化交易的理解也是提升市场稳定性的重要组成部分。可通过投资者教育基地、媒体平台
与高校合作等方式，向投资者普及量化交易的基本原理、策略类别、收益来源与风险特征，
帮助投资者形成更理性的预期。同时，券商、基金公司等销售机构在产品推介中需加强适
当性管理，对采用量化策略的产品提供更清晰的策略说明。随着投资者对量化交易的理
解逐步深化，市场在情绪扰动下的同步化反应将有所减弱，有助于缓解极端行情中的风险
共振。

屈源育等：量化交易与金融市场稳定
Quantitative Trading and Financial Market Stability:
An Analysis Based on Tick-by-tick Order Data
QU Yuanyua, Lin William Congb,c and WU Weixingd
(a: China School of Banking and Finance, University of International Business and Economics;
b: Business School and College of Computing and Data Science, Nanyang Technological University;
c: Fudan International School of Finance;
d: Capital University of Economics and Business)
Summary: This paper examines how the expansion of quantitative trading influences financial stability in
the Chinese equity market. The academic debate over whether quantitative trading stabilizes or destabilizes
markets remains unresolved. Existing studies not only emphasize its roles in providing liquidity and improv‐
ing price discovery but also point to its tendency to induce strategy homogeneity and amplify market fragility.
China’s A-share market offers a distinctive setting for addressing this issue. It provides extensive transaction-
level data and has experienced rapid growth in quantitative trading within an evolving regulatory framework,
allowing for a detailed assessment of how algorithmic participation affects tail-risk dynamics.
Using all A-share stocks from 2015 to 2023, the paper identifies quantitative trading by analyzing high-
frequency order submissions, cancellations and executions, and constructs a stock-level index of quantitative
trading activity. Tail-risk contagion is measured using a co-kurtosis-based metric that distinguishes two direc‐
tions of extreme risk transmission. Market-to-stock contagion captures how sensitive individual stocks are to
system-wide shocks, while stock-to-market contagion reflects the extent to which shocks originating from in‐
dividual stocks spread to the broader market. To mitigate potential endogeneity, the analysis follows the em‐
pirical strategy in prior literature and employs a two-stage least squares approach using lagged nominal prices
as an instrument for quantitative trading activity.
The results show that quantitative trading has asymmetric effects on financial stability. First, greater
quantitative trading activity significantly reduces market-to-stock tail-risk contagion. During market down‐
turns, stocks with more active quantitative trading face a lower probability of extreme losses. This stabilizing
effect is consistent with quantitative traders supplying liquidity and accelerating price discovery, and it is
stronger when firm information is more reliable, when stock prices appear undervalued, or when investor sen‐
timent is low.
Second, quantitative trading increases stock-to-market tail-risk contagion. Stocks with higher quantita‐
tive trading activity are more likely to transmit their own extreme losses to the market, especially when mu‐
tual fund ownership is low or when short-horizon, high-volatility strategies dominate. The effect is also stron‐
ger when investor sentiment is elevated. Moreover, the role of quantitative trading depends on market condi‐
tions. Under sharp price pressure, its liquidity-stabilizing effect weakens while its contagion potential rises.
Under liquidity shortages, by contrast, quantitative traders tend to provide additional depth to the order book
and help alleviate short-term stress.
The paper further evaluates the impact of policy interventions related to quantitative trading. Enhanced
information-based supervision, such as more detailed reporting of strategies and positions and more timely
monitoring, appears effective in reducing bidirectional tail-risk contagion. Restrictions that do not differenti‐
ate among strategy types may reduce liquidity at certain times.
This study makes several contributions. First, it provides new evidence on the dual role of quantitative
trading as both a stabilizer and a transmitter of systemic stress in an emerging market with distinctive institu‐
tional characteristics. Second, by combining order-level data with directional measures of tail-risk contagion,
the study links micro-level trading behavior to macro-level financial stability concerns and offers a more com‐
prehensive framework than traditional metrics such as volatility or spreads. Third, the findings contribute to
global discussions on how to balance technological innovation, market efficiency, and systemic risk manage‐
ment, and provide a basis for future research on high-frequency trading, cross-market contagion, and the inter‐
action between algorithmic strategies and market design.
Keywords: Quantitative Trading; Program Trading; Algorithmic Trading; Financial Market Stability; Risk
Contagion
