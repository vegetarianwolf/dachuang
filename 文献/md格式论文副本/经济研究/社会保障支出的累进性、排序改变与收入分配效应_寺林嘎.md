五、排序改变的特征与来源
排序改变是指不同人群在社会中所处的相对收入位置发生变动。从社会保障
体系的运行机制看，社保支出引起收入排序改变是常见现象。原因在于社保项目
的资金分配并非仅以收入水平为依据。例如，养老金待遇的领取主要由年龄与参
保状态决定，医保报销更多取决于健康风险和医保支出，儿童补贴则与家庭结构相
关。因此，社保支出引起的排序变化可能与制度目标及资格规则有关。与此同时，
若制度设计存在缺陷或运行管理不当，排序改变可能偏离政策预期。比如，部分群
体待遇过高会大幅提升其收入位置；而覆盖面缺口则使相关群体无法获得足够的
社会保障，在排序中处于不利地位。因此，考察社会保障支出对居民收入排序的影
响，重点在于分析排序改变的特征和来源。

六、结论与政策含义

社会保障支出是政府实施收入再分配、保障民生与促进经济社会发展的重要
政策工具。然而，关于我国社会保障支出的收入分配效应，现有研究结论存在明显
分歧。本文利用 年中国家庭收入调查数据，基于统一的再分配效应分析框架，
2023
系统评估了社保支出的收入分配效应。研究发现，我国社会保障支出总体上降低
了居民收入不平等程度，但对其累进性的评价依赖于收入参照基准的选取。若以
政府转移前收入为基准，社保支出呈现累进性；若以转移后收入为基准，则呈现累
退性。排序改变是解释上述累进性分歧的关键。社保支出政策实施后，居民收入
排序发生了显著变化，导致低收入群体的界定对收入基准选择较为敏感。进一步
分析表明，基本养老保险制度间待遇差距是排序改变的重要原因。
基于以上研究结论，本文认为在分析社保支出的再分配效应时，选择以转移前
还是转移后收入排序为基准，本质上反映了关于“何种排序更加合理”的不同价值
判断，并无孰优孰劣之分。以转移前排序为基准，侧重回答相较于初始收入分布，
现行政策是否有助于缩小收入差距，以及资源是否向初始收入较低的群体倾斜。
以转移后排序为基准，则以政策实施后的收入分布为起点，强调新增支出能否瞄准
最终意义上的低收入者，侧重于讨论政策扩张的潜在效应。在评估社会保障以及
其他再分配政策的政策效果时，两种排序基准提供了互补的视角。因此，理解我国
社保支出的收入分配效应，意味着既要肯定现行社保制度缩小收入差距的成效，也
要认识到，若按照现有资金分配格局，单纯扩大支出规模反而可能加剧收入不平
等，因此需重点优化社保资金的分配方式。
本文研究具有清晰的政策含义，主要包括以下四点：第一，缩小不同基本养老

支出再分配功能的关键抓手，进一步健全统筹城乡、公平统一的社会保障体系。
其一，首要任务是逐步提高城乡居民基础养老金水平，缩小居民保与职工保之间
的待遇差距。以充分发挥养老保险体系在保障全体老年人基本生活、防范老年贫

的公平性。
第二，健全城镇职工基本养老保险待遇的合理调整机制。城镇退休职工基本
养老金经过多年较快增长，待遇水平持续提升，但现行待遇调整机制在启动条件、
调整幅度与分配取向等方面仍存在一定的政策裁量空间，导致规则的可预期性与
透明度有待提高。《中共中央关于制定国民经济和社会发展第十五个五年规划的建
议》明确指出，要健全养老保险待遇确定和调整机制。退休人员的待遇调整应统筹
考虑工资增长、通货膨胀率与经济发展状况等因素，既要尽力而为，确保养老金购
买力不下降，也要量力而行，统筹当前和长远，避免待遇过快增长带来养老基金压
力与财政负担上升。通过完善待遇调整机制，形成稳定预期，并在分配规则上进一
步向低收入群体倾斜，从而更好地发挥社会保障在调节收入分配、扩大内需与促进
社会公平方面的积极作用。
第三，推动基本养老保险高质量扩面，并完善和落实全国统筹制度。随着制度
体系逐步完善，我国养老保险覆盖面不断扩大，有助于提升保障的普惠性，从而改
善收入分配格局。然而，在就业形态快速变化的背景下，越来越多的劳动者难以被
传统的、以稳定劳动关系为基础的参保机制有效覆盖，部分群体参保积极性偏弱，
或更多转向城乡居民养老保险。与此同时，人口跨区域流动加剧及地区间发展的

共担能力。为此，一方面，要优化参保与缴费规则，全面取消在就业地参保的户籍
限制，积极推动灵活就业人员、农民工和新就业形态人员高质量参保。另一方面，
应在完善全国统筹制度框架下，统一规范相关政策与待遇标准，完善养老保险关系
转移接续政策，为劳动力更充分流动提供制度保障。同时，应健全基金调剂与风险
分担机制，增强养老保险制度的稳定性和可持续性。
第四，完善退休制度，积极应对人口老龄化。本文研究发现，部分城镇退休职

期视角下推动合理的排序调整，也可能因制度设计不完善，导致偏离政策目标的非
预期排序变化。如何在经验层面区分两类排序变化，讨论其福利含义与政策含义，
也是未来值得努力的方向。

寺林嘎等：社会保障支出的累进性、排序改变与收入分配效应
Progressivity Versus Reranking: Understanding the Redistributive
Effect of Social Security Expenditure
SI Lin’gaa,b, WANG Zhonghuac and BAI Yanfengd
(a: School of Public Finance and Taxation, Inner Mongolia University of Finance and Economics;
b: School of Finance, Renmin University of China;
c: School of Public Finance and Taxation, Zhongnan University of Economics and Law;
d: School of Public Finance and Taxation, Central University of Finance and Economics)
Summary: Income inequality is a paramount concern for governments worldwide because it may have sig‐
nificant consequences for economic growth and social stability. Social security expenditure functions both
as a social safety net and as a primary instrument for income redistribution. Since the launch of reform and
opening up, China has progressively built a social security system encompassing social insurance, social as‐
sistance, and social welfare. At present, China has established the world’s largest social security system.
Basic old-age insurance is the main component of the social security system, consisting of the basic old-age
insurance for urban employees and that for urban and rural residents, with a total coverage of approximately
1.07 billion people. Despite these achievements, the literature remains divided on whether social security ex‐
penditure in China reduces income inequality. This paper seeks to clarify the sources of this debate and to
provide credible empirical evidence and interpretation. More broadly, limited redistributive capacity and
low spending efficiency are common challenges for social security systems in many developing countries.
Accordingly, our analysis also offers important policy implications for improving the effectiveness of redis‐
tributive instruments and promoting more inclusive development.
Using data from the China Household Income Project, we estimate the redistributive effect of social se‐
curity expenditure. We implement the decompositions proposed by Kakwani (1984) and Lerman & Yitzhaki
(1995) to examine progressivity and the extent of reranking. We further develop a household-level rerank‐
ing measure to quantify the contribution of institutional features in China’s pension system to reranking.
We document three key findings. First, the overall redistributive effect of social security expenditure is posi‐
tive, but its progressivity depends on the reference income. It is progressive when households are ranked by
pre-transfer income, but regressive when ranked by post-transfer income. Second, social security expendi‐
ture has a substantial reranking effect. Consequently, the composition of the low-income group differs mark‐
edly across reference income rankings, which in turn affects assessments of progressivity. Third, reranking
is driven primarily by pensions. Differences in benefit levels across basic old-age insurance programs are a
key institutional source of reranking.
This paper contributes to the existing literature in three ways. First, it deepens understanding of the dis‐
tributive impact of China’s social security expenditure by offering new evidence and a coherent explana‐
tion. Although the existing literature is extensive, its conclusions remain inconsistent. Using a unified de‐
composition framework, we show that assessments of progressivity hinge on the reference income used for
ranking, which helps explain why prior studies reach different conclusions. Second, we extend the analysis
of redistributive policies by highlighting reranking. Redistribution affects not only income levels but also
households’ relative positions in the income distribution. Prior work typically treats reranking as a residual
component in decompositions and provides limited evidence on its patterns and determinants. We offer a
systematic analysis of reranking induced by social security expenditure, thereby clarifying its interpretation
and the mechanisms through which redistribution policies operate. Third, we enrich the literature on the re‐
distributive effect of pensions. We decompose the reranking effect of pensions into contributions from
between-program benefit gaps, within-program benefit gaps, and incomplete coverage, offering a new per‐
spective for evaluating the distributional consequences of pension systems.
Keywords: Social Security Expenditure; Pensions; Progressivity; Reranking
