六、结论与政策启示
最低工资标准调整机制是收入分配制度的重要内容，深入考察最低工资调整
对企业行为的影响，对优化政策设计具有重要意义。本文研究结果表明，最低工资
每上涨 ，新设间接用工服务商数量将显著增加 。同时，最低工资上涨显
10% 2.88%
著减少了间接用工服务商的注册资本，使间接用工服务商趋向小型化，并且显著增
加了性质为个体工商户的间接用工服务商设立。从异质性上看，一方面，相比劳动
者承担闲时劳动成本的新型间接用工服务商，最低工资上涨显著减少了传统间接
用工服务商设立数量。另一方面，经济不确定性越高、劳动关系监管越严格，最低
工资促进间接用工服务商设立的作用越大。研究还发现，最低工资上涨会促使企
业分工细化、企业规模缩小。在最低工资上涨对就业产生负向影响的情况下，间接
用工服务商的增加有助于提高就业水平，在一定程度上缓解了最低工资对就业的
冲击。
结合本文的理论机制和实证结果，可以得到以下五点政策启示。第一，把握最
低工资上涨推动市场分工深化的趋势，积极引导生产性服务业向专业化、高端化发
展。研究结果表明，为应对用工成本上升，企业正从传统的直接雇用转向购买专业
化人力资源服务，这为生产性服务业创造了新的发展空间。合理的劳动保护政策
不仅促使企业自发进行效率改进，也有助于推动产业链专业化分工，符合高质量发

展的内在要求。但需要注意的是，最低工资上涨对间接用工服务商的促进作用在
人力资本水平较低的地区更为明显，反映出当前其对生产性服务业的推动仍主要
集中在劳动密集型服务领域。这与“十五五”规划建议中分领域推进生产性服务业
向专业化和价值链高端环节延伸的目标仍存在一定差距。因此，投资于人的必要
性和紧迫性更加凸显。尤其在人力资本基础相对薄弱的地区，应进一步加大职业
技能培训与公共服务投入，着力推动生产性服务业从劳动密集型向技能支撑型转
变，逐步夯实产业升级的人力资源基础。
第二，正视新型用工形态对劳动者权益保障带来的挑战，加快构建适配多元化
就业的社会保障和劳动监管体系。近年来，政府出台了一系列政策文件，如国务院
办公厅发布的《关于促进平台经济规范健康发展的指导意见》和人力资源和社会保
障部、国家发展改革委等八部门发布的《关于维护新就业形态劳动者劳动保障权益
的指导意见》，反映了党和国家对发展新就业形态的高度关注、支持和引导。研究
发现，最低工资上涨推动用工形式向平台化、灵活化转型，传统的以标准劳动关系
为基础的社会保险和劳动监察制度难以有效保障劳动者权益。因此，完善劳动者
权益保障制度，探索建立以劳动者而非劳动关系为中心的权益保障新模式至关重
要。在社会保障方面，应加快扩大新就业形态人员职业伤害保障试点；在劳动监察
方面，应强化对平台企业的穿透式监管，利用数字化技术核查其算法规则、报酬支
付的合规性，推动建立劳资协商机制。
第三，关注间接用工服务商小型化、个体化的结构性变化，完善面向小微市场
主体的法律规范与扶持体系。研究结果表明，最低工资上涨促使新设间接用工服
务商趋于小型化和个体工商户化。一方面，市场准入门槛降低、创业活力迸发，符
合国家鼓励小微企业和个体工商户发展的方向。但另一方面，大量小微主体的涌
入也带来了规范经营水平、抗风险能力、法律责任意识等方面的现实挑战，可能引
发市场无序竞争并损害劳动者权益。因此，政策需扶管结合，精准发力。一方面，
市场监管、税务、人社部门应协同优化针对个体工商户等小微市场主体的一站式开
办、报税、社保代办服务，并探索提供小额信贷、创业培训等针对性扶持。另一方
面，应加强普法宣传与合规指导，通过案例教学、线上课程等方式，提升其依法用
工、规范管理的意识和能力。
第四，强化政策制定的区域协同与全国统筹，在建设全国统一大市场的过程中
促进就业。本文基于省界相邻城市对样本，识别出最低工资上涨显著增加了新设
间接用工服务商数量，这一因果证据进一步揭示：在经济发展阶段相似、禀赋相近
的相邻地区，若采取各自为政的政策，虽可短期内塑造本地市场结构，却也可能诱
发区域间对市场主体与产业资源的逐底竞争或政策套利，加剧市场分割，阻碍要素
在更大范围内的有效配置。因此，建议在国家层面推动建立省际政策协商沟通机
制，对最低工资等具有外部性的劳动政策，在调整时序、力度上进行跨区域协调与
引导，支持毗邻地区开展政策试点联动。
第五，以深入推进数字中国建设为契机，提升劳动力市场监测与服务水平。

另一方面为中小企业提供合规自检、服务商筛选等数字化工具，降低其制度性交

马 双等：用工成本上涨与企业间接用工
Rising Labor Costs and Labor Subcontracting: A Perspective from
the Development of Producer Services
MA Shuanga, LAI Mantonga,b and SUN Churenc
(a: School of Economics and Statistics, Guangzhou University;
b: Shenzhen Bao’an District Commerce Bureau;
c: School of Economics and Trade, Guangdong University of Foreign Studies)
Summary: The strengthening of labor protection systems constrains firms’ ability to adjust employment
and wages, thereby increasing labor cost rigidity. Minimum wage regulations not only set a lower bound on
wages but also raise a range of policy-induced costs, such as social insurance contributions. Under these
conditions, firms seek more flexible employment arrangements to alleviate cost pressures. By relying on la‐
bor subcontracting through third-party service providers, firms can transform rigid labor costs into variable
costs, facilitate wage differentiation, reduce employment risks, and partially circumvent policy-related ex‐
penditures, thus reducing costs and improving efficiency.
Because micro-level survey data do not allow for a direct measurement of the scale of labor subcon‐
tracting, existing studies mainly focus on firms’ short-term direct hiring behavior and pay limited attention
to how labor costs affect labor subcontracting and the development of producer services. This study pio‐
neers a theoretical and empirical investigation into how rising labor costs promote labor subcontracting
from the perspective of the establishment of labor subcontracting service providers. The theoretical analysis
shows that rising labor costs enhance the bargaining power of labor subcontracting service providers and
raise the share of subcontracted workers within firms, which in turn stimulates the entry of such providers
and promotes the development of producer services.
Using China’s business registration data (2001-2021), city-level minimum wage data (2000-2020), and
urban macroeconomic data,the empirical analysis yields the following findings. First, a 10% increase in the
minimum wage leads to a statistically significant 2.88% increase in the number of newly established labor
subcontracting service providers, a result that is robust to multiple robustness checks. Second, minimum
wage increases are associated with a reduction in the average scale of labor subcontracting service providers
and a shift toward individually owned firms; Third, heterogeneity analysis indicates that the effect of mini‐
mum wage increases on the establishment of labor subcontracting service providers is stronger in environ‐
ments with higher economic uncertainty and stricter labor regulation. For manufacturing firms, labor cost
savings constitute the primary motivation for adopting labor subcontracting. Fourth, employment pattern ad‐
justments induced by minimum wage increases lead to a finer division of labor across firms and a smaller
scale of newly registered firms. The expansion of labor subcontracting service providers mitigates the ad‐
verse employment effects of minimum wage increases.
This study extends the existing literature by making three main contributions. First, it proposes an inno‐
vative measurement approach by constructing indicators based on the entry of new labor subcontracting ser‐
vice providers, thereby addressing measurement challenges in labor subcontracting and shedding light on
the development of producer services such as labor outsourcing and human resource services. Second, it de‐
velops a theoretical model that explicitly incorporates interactions among manufacturing firms, labor sub‐
contracting service providers, and workers, highlighting the intermediary role of service providers and, for
the first time, integrating heterogeneous employment arrangements into the analysis of the minimum wage-
employment relationship. Third, it examines employment pattern adjustments from the perspective of the
registered capital size of newly established firms, and explores their implications for inter-firm division of
labor and the development of producer services. Moreover, the study incorporates labor subcontracting ser‐
vice providers into the empirical analysis of the conventional minimum wage-employment relationship, re‐
vealing the interactive effects of minimum wage increases and labor subcontracting on employment out‐
comes.
Keywords: Minimum Wage; Labor Subcontracting; Labor Cost Rigidity; Producer Services
